Structural Drying Services in Tewksbury, MA

What the equipment does, why drying takes days and how a crew knows the building is dry.

By the Tewksbury Water Damage Pros team · 2026

☎ Call (978) 252-8637

Structural drying services in Tewksbury, MA mean removing moisture from the parts of a building you cannot see: studs, subfloor, wall cavities, insulation and the space behind cabinets. Towels and a box fan dry the surface. Structural drying uses measured airflow, dehumidification and daily readings to reach a target.

That target is a dry standard, based on unaffected material in the same home. Crews do not stop when the surface feels dry. They stop when the meter matches the reference, which is why a job can run for several days.

Why drying takes days, not hours

Water soaks into wood, drywall and plaster much faster than it leaves. Moisture has to move from deep inside the material to the surface and into the air, and the air has to be dry enough to take it. Push too hard and wood cracks or cups. Push too softly and mold gets a head start.

The Massachusetts DPH mold guide says wet areas should be completely dried in 24 hours, and under the state housing code areas exposed to leaks or floods must be dried within 48 hours. The EPA says the same window, 24 to 48 hours, usually prevents mold growth. Equipment is how crews hit that window.

How crews set up a dry-out

  1. Find the water

    Meters and a thermal imaging camera map where the water went, not just where it shows.

  2. Extract

    Extraction units pull standing water. Soaked carpet pad and loose insulation come out.

  3. Open up

    Baseboards come off, and small holes may be made so air can get into cavities.

  4. Place equipment

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers go in by room volume and wetness, not by guess.

  5. Record a baseline

    Readings and humidity are logged so progress can be seen.

Drying goals and daily monitoring

The goal is to return materials to their normal moisture content. Crews pick a dry standard from unaffected wood or drywall in the same house, then compare each day. S500 is the published standard for water damage restoration, and homeowners can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Each visit, the crew rereads the marked spots, checks humidity, adjusts air movers and removes equipment from areas that have reached goal. A job with no daily readings is a job you cannot verify.

What you should and should not do with the equipment

  • Do keep the equipment running, including overnight. Noise is part of the process.
  • Do keep doors and windows closed in the drying zone so the dehumidifiers work on the right air.
  • Do not unplug a machine to charge a phone or use the outlet. Ask the crew about a different outlet.
  • Do not move air movers or point them at furniture. Placement is by design.
  • Do not turn off a dehumidifier because the room feels dry. The meters decide.
  • Do not open a wall or lift a floor yourself to check. Let the crew do it.
Electrical safety

Equipment needs power, and cords across wet floors are a hazard. If water is near an outlet or panel, turn off the breaker first and have an electrician check before the crew plugs anything in.

How to tell drying is finished

Finished means the meter readings at the marked spots match the dry standard, humidity in the room has returned to normal, and no thermal imaging hot or cold spots remain. The crew should show you the logged readings. Surface feel, a lack of smell or a warm room are not proof.

What affects the number of drying days

Drying time depends on the water category, the area affected, the materials, how long the water sat, outdoor humidity, and whether cavities and layers like pad, subfloor or plaster hold water. Local note

The Massachusetts drying rule in plain terms

Under the Massachusetts housing code, areas exposed to leaks or floods must be dried within 48 hours. The state DPH mold guide goes further and says wet areas should be completely dried in 24 hours. The practical reading: begin drying at once and aim to have the reachable structure dry inside those two days.

So start drying the same day, keep dated photos and the crew's readings, and ask your insurer what your policy requires. Mold-affected areas of more than 10 square feet are a job for professionals.
