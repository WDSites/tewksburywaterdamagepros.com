How to Get Insurance to Pay for Water Damage in Tewksbury, MA

A step-by-step claim and documentation guide for homeowners with a fresh water loss.

By the Tewksbury Water Damage Pros team · 2026

☎ Call (978) 252-8637

If you want to know how to get insurance to pay for water damage in Tewksbury, MA, start with three things: a stopped leak, photos taken before cleanup, and your policy open to the declarations page. Insurers decide claims on evidence and policy wording. Your job in the first day is to build a clean paper trail while the damage is still visible.

No guide can promise a claim will be approved, and we do not decide coverage. What we can lay out is the order that makes a claim easier for an adjuster to read: protect, document, report, get a written scope, and know your options if the answer is no.

Stop the water and protect what is left

Most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage after a loss. Close the main valve, move furniture and electronics out of the wet zone, and tarp any roof or window opening. Cut power to a wet room only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. Read your policy wording on this point, and keep every receipt.

Hold off on ripping out soaked drywall or carpet. Once material is in a dumpster, the proof of how far the water traveled goes with it.

The documentation checklist, in order

Work through this list top to bottom. Each item takes minutes, and together they are the file an adjuster will ask for.

  1. Photograph the source of the water first: the split supply line, the stained ceiling, the backed-up drain.
  2. Take wide shots of every affected room, then close-ups of floors, baseboards, walls and ceilings with a tape measure beside the damage for scale.
  3. Record a slow video walk-through and say the date out loud.
  4. List damaged belongings room by room: item, rough age, and replacement cost if you know it.
  5. Photograph labels, model plates and serial numbers on appliances and electronics before anything is thrown out.
  6. Save every receipt: emergency plumber, tarps, equipment, hotel nights if the house is unlivable.
  7. Log each call with the date, time, name of the person, and any claim number.
  8. Ask the restoration company for its moisture readings, drying logs and a written scope of work.

Copy the photos to cloud storage the same day. A phone that ends up in a flooded basement helps nobody.

Call your insurer and read what you are signing

Policies set notice duties, and the wording differs from one to the next. Find the section on what to do after a loss and follow it. Report the claim promptly instead of waiting to see how bad things get. Many insurers run claims lines around the clock, so you can open a claim before repairs are scoped.

Pull out the declarations page next. It shows your deductible, your dwelling and contents limits, and any endorsements. The deductible is the amount you carry before payment starts, so when damage looks close to that figure, a precise scope matters even more. Ask the claims representative to explain, in plain terms, what happens after the adjuster visit.

Read before you sign

Check what any paper from a contractor or an insurer actually says before signing it. If a form assigns your claim rights or directs payment to someone else, ask your insurer what that means for you first.

Get a written scope from the restoration company

A useful scope names each room, the water category, the materials removed, the equipment placed and the expected drying days. The adjuster compares it with their own inspection, so gaps get settled on paper. Ask any company whether its crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500 and whether moisture readings are recorded at every visit.

Our water damage cleanup work produces that room-by-room record, and the drying side of the job is logged under water mitigation with daily readings. If the adjuster questions an item, those readings are your answer.

When the adjuster asks to see damaged materials

Photograph the item, then keep it. A cut section of wet drywall, a swollen cabinet panel or a split supply hose can sit in a bagged corner of the garage until the claim moves. If a piece is too big to store, such as soaked carpet, ask the adjuster whether photos and a small cut sample will do.

Ask before the dumpster fills, not after.

If the claim is denied or comes in lower than expected

Ask for the reason in writing, with the policy language the insurer relied on. Then compare that clause with your declarations page and any endorsements. Ask the insurer how its appeals or reconsideration process works and how to send new evidence, such as drying logs or a plumber's report on the cause of the leak.

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance has a consumer service unit that takes questions about insurance problems, and its website is the place to start. Keep copies of everything you send. We cannot decide coverage or predict an outcome, but a tidy file helps whoever reviews it.

Local note

What Tewksbury homeowners should know

If the loss means rebuilding walls, plumbing or electrical, ask the Tewksbury Building Department (1009 Main Street, Lower Level) whether your repair needs a permit. Permits go through the Town's online portal. Put the answer in your claim file, since the rebuild scope your adjuster sees may need to match the work actually done.

Tewksbury sewer charges are based on water consumption, so a leak that runs through the meter can raise that part of the bill as well. Ask the Town Water and Sewer billing office whether any adjustment applies. Nothing is certain, so ask rather than assume, and keep the bill with your claim papers.

The paperwork steps are the same across town lines. We have pages for water damage restoration in Andover and Billerica if your damaged property sits in either town.
