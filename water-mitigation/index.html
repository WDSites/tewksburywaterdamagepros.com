While you wait for a crew, shut off the main water valve, cut power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and easy to reach, lift valuables off wet floors and keep children and pets out of the room.

The published standard for this work is ANSI/IICRC S500, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Water mitigation is the work that stops the damage from growing. It comes before cleanup and long before repair, and it is built around one idea: remove the water and dry the structure fast enough that most of the building materials survive.

Wet walls, soft floors and a musty smell: what mitigation is for

Most people do not search for mitigation. They search for what they notice: a wall that feels cool and damp, carpet that squishes underfoot, a ceiling stain that keeps growing or a musty smell in a room that was fine last week.

Those are all signs that water is sitting inside materials you cannot see. Water mitigation is the trade name for finding it, removing it and drying the building until readings come back to normal. That is the difference between a room that dries out and a room that grows mold.

When wet materials become an emergency

The Massachusetts Department of Public Health says wet areas should be dried completely within 24 hours, and the state housing code requires areas exposed to leaks or floods to be dried within 48 hours. The US EPA also points to 24 to 48 hours as the window for preventing mold growth.

That clock is why we answer at any hour. A burst pipe at 2 a.m. is still a 2 a.m. problem, and waiting for business hours costs drying time.

Safety before anything else

If water has reached outlets, light fixtures or the electrical panel, stay out of the wet area and call the utility or 911 about any electrical or gas risk before you call us. Do not walk through standing water near live power.

How we mitigate: the working sequence

  1. Stop the source

    Close the main or the local shutoff, or confirm the plumber has. Nothing dries while water is still arriving.

  2. Remove standing water

    Extraction units pull water from floors and carpet. Fast removal does more for drying than any equipment that follows.

  3. Map the moisture

    Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras show where water traveled behind walls, under floors and above ceilings.

  4. Set the drying system

    Air movers push air across wet surfaces and LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture from the air. Equipment placement follows the moisture map.

  5. Monitor until dry

    Readings are taken over several days. Equipment comes out only when materials reach their dry target.

Structural drying

Structural drying is the equipment-heavy part of mitigation, and it is why a crew returns for several days. Wall cavities, subfloors and framing hold moisture long after the surface feels dry, so we measure instead of guessing.

Drywall that stayed wet for a short time can often be dried in place. Insulation, carpet pad and soaked ceiling panels usually cannot, and are removed so the cavity can dry. We explain which is which before anything is removed.

What affects the scope and price

Mitigation scope is set after an inspection, and the price follows the scope. The same leak can be a one-room job or a whole-floor job depending on how far the water traveled.

  • Water category (clean, gray or contaminated) and how long it has been there
  • Affected area and the number of rooms
  • Material types, from carpet and pad to plaster and hardwood
  • Drying days, which set equipment time
  • Whether demolition is needed to reach wet cavities
