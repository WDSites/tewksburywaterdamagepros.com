Tewksbury Center is the old village core of town. The Massachusetts Historical Commission's 1980 town report describes Main Street as the geographic center of Tewksbury, with a well-preserved village common and Victorian houses, and later building spread onto the side streets.

What the Town Center looks like on the ground

The same state report says the 1737 meetinghouse stood at Main and East Streets, and that roads fan out from the center along North, East, Pine and Rogers Streets. The Town's open space plan also describes a Town Center Overlay District meant to support mixed-use, walkable development.

Houses of different ages sit close together here, so the cause and the materials change from one address to the next. We do not assume how old your house is. We ask, and we check. Neighbors to the north and south have their own pages, for North Tewksbury and South Tewksbury.

Water in older and newer houses near Main Street

Older houses often have plaster walls and ceilings, and wet plaster can be dried in place when the problem is caught early. Newer drywall usually cannot be saved once it has soaked through. A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera show which one you have, wall by wall.

If a supply line splits, a washing machine hose lets go or a water heater leaks, the first job is the same: stop the source, then remove standing water with extraction units. Our water mitigation page covers the drying stage, and water damage repair covers the rebuild once the structure is dry.

Wet ground in and around the center

The Town's stormwater drainage inventory lists a wetland running through Tewksbury Center, with ten outfalls, and another wetland near Main Street and Salem Road with eight. That is a drainage fact, not a flood history for any street, and we do not claim any address is flood prone.

If water comes through a basement wall or floor after heavy rain, tell us. Groundwater and runoff are treated as contaminated under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, unlike a clean pipe leak. See basement water damage.

Safety steps in the first hour

  1. Shut off the water at the main valve if a pipe is the source.
  2. Do not touch the breaker panel, switches or outlets while you are standing in water. Call an electrician or your utility if the panel is wet.
  3. Keep children and pets out of the wet area.
  4. If the water came from a sewer or drain backup, or from outdoor flooding, do not run fans and do not try to clean it up yourself.
  5. Take photos and short video of the damage before anything is moved.

Insurance paperwork and what we document

Whether a loss is covered depends on the cause and on your policy. Sudden pipe or appliance failures are often treated differently from slow leaks, and flood usually needs its own coverage. We do not decide coverage and we do not promise it.

We write down the affected rooms, take photos, record moisture readings and list what was removed, so you have records for your insurer.
