Warm floor spot, running meter, or the sound of water? What it means

A slab leak is a break in a pipe that runs in or under a concrete floor. You rarely see it. You notice what the water does around it.

  • A warm patch on the floor that does not match the rest of the room
  • A water meter that keeps turning with every fixture and appliance off
  • A faint hiss or trickle when the house is quiet
  • Carpet edges or wall bottoms that stay damp for no clear reason

Any one sign can have a duller cause, like a dripping fixture. Several together point at the floor. A licensed plumber finds the pipe, and we handle the wet materials. Our water mitigation page explains that stage.

Where slab leaks actually turn up in Tewksbury homes

Since most New England homes have basements, concrete floors cluster in a few places. Think of a ranch-style house on a single pad, a garage slab with a water line through it, and an addition built on grade off an older house. Radiant heat tubing and supply lines run under the slab to a bathroom are the other real sources.

We have no Tewksbury count of slab homes and will not guess. If you are unsure what your house sits on, ask the Town of Tewksbury Building Department what plans exist for your address.

First-hour safety steps for a suspected slab leak

  1. Close the main shutoff and check the meter. If the dial stops, the leak is on your side.
  2. Switch off breakers near wet floors only from dry ground. Otherwise call your electric utility or 911.
  3. Keep people off a warm or soft floor, since hot lines can scald.
  4. Do not drill or core the slab to hunt for the pipe. You can hit another line or a radiant loop.
  5. Photograph the damp areas and the meter, then call a licensed plumber, then us.

The Mass.gov plumbers and gas fitters consumer fact sheet says Massachusetts law prohibits anyone but a licensed professional from repairing plumbing. The Tewksbury Building Department FAQ adds that homeowners cannot secure plumbing permits, so let your plumber apply.

How we dry a floor that sits on a concrete slab

Concrete holds moisture a long time, so we test before we trust what we feel. A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera map where the water traveled.

  1. Map the wet zone

    Readings across floor, lower drywall and cabinet bases set a baseline.

  2. Remove what will not dry

    Saturated pad, loose baseboard and swollen toe kicks come out first.

  3. Open the wall bottoms

    Small cuts let air reach wet drywall and insulation.

  4. Dry the air and surfaces

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run, with mats for heavy flooring.

  5. Recheck and release

    Equipment leaves when readings return to a dry target.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard, and you can ask any company whether its crews follow it.

Insurance and a slab leak: what to ask

Sudden and accidental plumbing discharge is often covered, while a gradual leak that went unnoticed often is not. Some policies treat the pipe and the resulting damage differently, so check your policy and ask your insurer before you tear anything out. We cannot promise approval or a payout.

The Town Water and Sewer Division bases sewer charges on water consumption, so a leak through the meter can raise that part of the bill. Ask the Town billing office whether any adjustment applies.

Mistakes to avoid and ways to catch a leak sooner

  • Do not rely on one fan to dry the room
