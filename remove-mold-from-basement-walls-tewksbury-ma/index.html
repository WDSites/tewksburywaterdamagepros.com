How to Remove Mold From Basement Walls in Tewksbury, MA

A DIY-or-pro guide built on the Massachusetts Department of Public Health homeowner mold guide.

By the Tewksbury Water Damage Pros team · 2026

☎ Call (978) 252-8637

To remove mold from basement walls in Tewksbury, MA, fix the water problem first, then clean hard surfaces with detergent and water. The Massachusetts Department of Public Health says professionals should handle mold-affected areas larger than 10 square feet. Porous materials such as drywall, insulation and carpet get removed, not cleaned.

That rule of thumb splits most basements into two groups: a small patch on painted block that a homeowner can handle, and wall systems with hidden or widespread growth that need a crew.

Fix the moisture first

Mold will keep growing until the water source is dealt with. The state guide separates acute problems, such as a burst pipe or sewer backup, from chronic ones like slow leaks, clogged gutters and high humidity. Look for a downspout dumping at the foundation, a dripping valve, a dehumidifier that has not been emptied or a condensate line that leaks.

Scrubbing a wall that is still damp is wasted effort. Get a moisture meter reading or at least check by touch, and fix the source before any cleaning starts.

DIY or pro: where the line falls

A small patch of visible mold on a hard surface, under 10 square feet, is within reach of a careful homeowner. The guide also advises that people with asthma or weakened immune systems should not do the cleanup. Hidden mold behind paneling, inside a wall or under flooring is a reason to call.

Call when the area is larger than 10 square feet, when it keeps returning to the same spot, when the basement took in sewage or flood water, or when a lender wants mold cleared before a sale. Our mold remediation crews work to ANSI/IICRC S520, and your questions about health belong with a physician.

No routine testing

The state guide does not recommend routine mold testing. If you can see mold, clean it and fix the moisture source. A test result does not replace either step.

Cleaning hard surfaces step by step

  1. Put on gloves, eye protection and an N95-type mask, and wear clothes you can wash or toss.
  2. Contain the area. Close doors, seal the doorway with plastic sheeting and run a window fan exhausting outdoors, so dust does not drift upstairs.
  3. Do not dry-brush or sweep. Mist the surface lightly so spores stay down.
  4. Scrub with detergent and water, working from the edges inward.
  5. If you choose bleach, mix no more than 1 cup of laundry bleach per gallon of water. Never mix bleach with ammonia.
  6. Wipe and rinse, then bag all rags and removed material tightly before leaving the basement.
  7. Dry the wall within 24 to 48 hours with fans and a dehumidifier.

Disturbing painted surfaces counts too. The state guide says working on more than 6 square feet of painted interior surface triggers lead-safe renovation rules, so stop and ask a pro if you plan heavy scraping.

What a professional adds

A mold crew sets up containment barriers, runs HEPA air scrubbers, removes porous material and cleans framing with HEPA vacuums and damp wiping. Then LGR dehumidifiers and air movers dry the space while moisture meters confirm the readings. The sequence follows S520.

Mold needs moisture, so the job is only as durable as the drying and the repair behind it. If water came in during a storm or from a sewer backup, flood damage restoration usually comes first.

How to vet a mold company

Massachusetts does not require a license for mold remediation, so you do the vetting. Ask about experience, references and complaint history. Ask whether the crew works to S520 and request a written scope. Pay in installments and hold the final payment until you have checked the work.

Because the state does not license mold work, the call you make to a company is your only screening step, so use the questions above. If the cleanup involves rebuilding a basement wall, ask the Tewksbury Building Department whether your repair needs a permit before the drywall goes back up.
