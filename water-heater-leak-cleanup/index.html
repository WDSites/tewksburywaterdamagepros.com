Emergency Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Tewksbury, MA

A leaking tank soaks the floor, the wall behind it and everything stored nearby. We extract the water and dry the space while a plumber handles the heater.

☎ Call (978) 252-8637

Emergency water heater leak cleanup in Tewksbury, MA starts with a simple picture: a tank or fitting that has been weeping for hours, and a basement corner, closet or utility room that is now wet. Hot water spreads across concrete, soaks the bottom of drywall and wicks into cardboard boxes faster than most people expect.

Our job is the water that got out. A licensed plumber repairs or replaces the heater, and we extract, dry and clean up around that work. If the leak is large or the floor is already flooded, our emergency water removal page explains how the first hours go.

Puddle by the tank, damp drywall, rusty streaks: what it means

A small puddle at the base of the heater can come from three places. A loose fitting on the supply or hot outlet drips slowly. A failing drain valve weeps at one spot. A corroded tank leaks from the bottom seam, and that kind of leak only gets bigger.

Look at the wall behind and beside the heater. Baseboard that has swelled, paint that bubbles near the floor and a musty smell in a closed utility room all point to water that has been there longer than the puddle suggests. When the water has reached framing, flooring or stored belongings, you need water damage cleanup, not just a mop.

Basement and utility-room leaks are easy to miss because nobody walks there daily. A damp patch behind a stack of boxes may have been growing for days. Gradual leaks like that can complicate an insurance claim, so note when you first saw the water.

First-hour safety steps for a leaking water heater

  1. Close the cold water supply valve on the pipe feeding the top of the heater. A quarter turn handle or a round wheel, either one stops the refill.
  2. For an electric heater, switch off its breaker at the panel. For a gas heater, turn the gas shutoff to the off position. Do not touch the panel if you are standing in water.
  3. Stay out of standing water that reaches outlets, the furnace, the panel or extension cords. Wait for the power to be off.
  4. Keep children and pets away. Water that left a tank can be hot enough to scald, and a hot floor stays slippery.
  5. Do not open the drain valve to speed things up, and do not touch the relief valve pipe.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 from outside. If the leak is on the street side of the meter, the Town of Tewksbury Water and Sewer Division handles the main and publishes its own emergency line on its web page.

Why the plumber comes first and we come right after

The Town of Tewksbury Building Department states that a homeowner is not allowed to secure permits for electrical or plumbing and gas work. A Massachusetts licensed plumber or gas fitter has to apply for them. In practice, that means you call a plumber for the heater and the permit, and you do not try a do-it-yourself swap.

The Massachusetts Plumbers and Gas Fitters consumer fact sheet adds a warning about parts such as the temperature and pressure relief valve. It says an improperly installed one can cause serious explosions and injuries. Ask any plumber you hire about license status, and check it with the state Board before work starts.

We do not replace heaters or fix plumbing. We schedule extraction and drying so the floor and walls are not still wet when the new tank goes in, and we leave the wet material in a clean state for the plumber to work around.

How we dry out a heater leak

  1. Find the wet extent

    A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera show how far water traveled under the wall, into the slab edge and into stored items.

  2. Extract standing water

    Portable extraction units pull water off concrete and low-pile flooring, then wet vacuums handle corners and thresholds.

  3. Remove what cannot dry

    Wet cardboard, soaked insulation, swollen baseboard and the bottom strip of saturated drywall come out so air can reach the cavity.

  4. Move air and pull humidity

    Air movers push dry air along the wall base while an LGR dehumidifier removes moisture from a closed utility space.

  5. Re-measure and document

    We take daily readings until the meter shows normal numbers, and we log them in writing for your records.

Water from a heater is normally clean supply water at first, which ANSI/IICRC S500 labels Category 1. It can degrade if it sits on dirty concrete or soaks contents for a day or two. That is why speed matters more than the size of the puddle.

Insurance and a water heater leak

Massachusetts Division of Insurance guidance describes covered water damage as sudden and accidental. A tank that bursts overnight often fits that description, while a drip that went unnoticed for months often does not. Read your policy and ask your insurer which category your leak falls into.

Call your insurer or agent soon, save every receipt, and do not make permanent repairs until the insurer has had a chance to inspect. If the water came from outside the house, such as rising groundwater, that is flood, and standard homeowners policies do not cover flood. A federal flood policy under the NFIP does list water heaters among covered building items in a basement, so a separate flood policy changes the picture. Check your declarations page.

Mistakes to avoid after a tank leak

Do not leave the cold supply open because the leak looks small, and do not close a wet wall with new drywall or paint. Spread wet boxes out instead of stacking them in a corner.

The EPA says drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours usually prevents mold growth. A plumber may need a day or more to schedule, so start drying now and let the heater swap follow. If dark spotting appears on the wall later, read about mold remediation.
