North Tewksbury is the northwest part of town, centered on Andover Street (Route 133). The Massachusetts Historical Commission's 1980 report says suburban development from Lowell reached this section and that few Colonial-period houses survive, which matches what you see on the street today.

The Town's master plan adds that many of the newer homes in North Tewksbury, and in the areas east and south of Tewksbury Hospital, are common-wall or multi-family units. A leak in a shared-wall building can reach a neighbor, and that shapes the first calls you make.

Shared walls and upstairs units

In attached housing, water often travels. A failed water heater or a dishwasher line in one unit can wet the ceiling of the unit below and the wall of the unit beside it. Tell the dispatcher whether neighbors are affected, and tell your association or property manager at the same time.

Drying runs through wall cavities, ceilings and subfloors. We use moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera to follow the water past the visible stain. The ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard is the published reference the drying follows. The drywall water damage repair page explains when wallboard can be dried and when it has to be cut out.

Wetlands, the Great Swamp and runoff

The Town's open space plan says the Heavy Industrial district begins in North Tewksbury and follows both sides of I-495 to the Great Swamp, the largest contiguous wetland system in town. The Great Swamp lies between North Street, Main Street and I-495.

A wetland nearby does not mean your home floods. It is a reason to check the drainage around your foundation, and a reason to ask us about groundwater if a basement takes water after a long rain. Read flood damage restoration for how outdoor water is treated differently from a pipe leak. If you are closer to Main Street, see Tewksbury Center.

Safety steps in the first hour

  1. Find the source and close the nearest shut-off or the main valve.
  2. Stay away from the electrical panel and outlets if you are standing in water or the panel is wet. Call an electrician or your utility.
  3. Do not use fans or try to clean up sewage or any water that came from outdoors.
  4. If a neighbor's unit is the source, contact the property manager and photograph the damage on your side.
  5. Move small valuables and rugs out of the wet area if the floor is safe to walk on.

Then call (978) 252-8637. Give your street, the floor, and whether the water is still arriving.

Who pays when the leak came from next door

Responsibility depends on where the water started, your association rules and your policy. We cannot assign blame and we do not decide coverage. We document the rooms, the moisture readings and the materials removed, so you have records to give your insurer or your association.

Ask your agent whether your policy covers the building, the contents, or both. Condominium and rental coverage often split that differently.

Cleanup and repair after drying

Once the readings are back to normal, the repair stage starts: replacing insulation and drywall, then paint and trim. Water damage repair covers that stage. We write the scope first so you know which finishes are in the plan.

Mold after a slow or hidden leak

Mold can grow behind a wall that stayed damp. If you notice a musty smell or dark spots days later, mention it. It is a separate scope, described on the mold remediation page.
