Kitchen Water Damage in Tewksbury, MA

Leaks under the sink and behind the dishwasher soak cabinets, subfloors and ceilings below. We dry them before you replace anything.

☎ Call (978) 252-8637

Kitchen water damage in Tewksbury, MA usually hides in a spot you cannot see: the base of the sink cabinet, the gap behind the dishwasher or the cavity under a toe-kick. A supply line weeps, and by the time the cabinet floor feels soft, the subfloor has been wet for days.

This page covers how crews dry kitchen leaks and what stays or goes. If the damage spread through the house, our water mitigation process applies to the whole level.

Soft cabinet floor, warped flooring, damp smell: what it means

A cabinet floor that dips when you press on it has absorbed water. A musty smell when you open the sink door means the cabinet is still holding moisture. Warped flooring near the dishwasher says the leak has reached the subfloor.

Kitchens hide leaks because everything is tight and enclosed. A drip from a faucet supply line runs down the back of the cabinet, into the floor, and sideways along the joists. Another spot you may not check is the ceiling in the room below, which can show a brown stain before anything looks wrong upstairs.

Callers often ask us to just look at it. That is a fair request, because moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera show what the eye cannot.

Safety steps before you touch the cabinets

  1. Close the angle stop valve under the sink. If it will not turn or still leaks, close the main water shutoff.
  2. Turn off the dishwasher and unplug it only if the outlet and floor around it are dry.
  3. Switch off the breaker for kitchen outlets if water reaches them. Do not touch a wet outlet.
  4. Empty the cabinet and move items to a dry spot so air can circulate.
  5. Take photos of the damage before cleaning.

If a ceiling below is bulging, keep people away from it. The Town of Tewksbury Water and Sewer Division handles leaks on the street side of the meter and publishes its own emergency line on its web page.

Who fixes the leak and who dries the kitchen

The Massachusetts Plumbers and Gas Fitters consumer fact sheet says replacing or relocating a valve or water supply or drain piping is not a minor repair. It needs a licensed plumber and a permit. So a corroded supply line or a failed drain trap goes to a plumber, and we do not claim to do that work.

The Town of Tewksbury Building Department FAQ quotes the state building code on ordinary repairs. It describes them as maintenance that does not affect structure, plumbing or other utilities. Because the Town page does not say more about plumbing permits, ask the Building Department before you start a rebuild.

Our work sits around the plumber's visit: extraction first, then drying, and a written moisture record the rebuild crew can use.

Drying cabinets, subfloors and flooring

Cabinets are dried through the toe-kick openings. We remove the toe kick, drill small holes where needed, and push dry air into the cavity with air movers. A thermal imaging camera confirms that the air reaches the back corners.

Toe kick and cabinet base

Removing the toe kick exposes the cavity. Particleboard cabinet bottoms swell and crumble, solid wood and plywood often dry and stay.

Subfloor

Plywood and OSB are read with a moisture meter. Damp subfloor is dried under panels or flooring that is lifted.

Laminate and engineered boards

These fail often. Their cores swell at the seams when wet, and the edges do not flatten again.

Countertop and cabinet decisions

We take cabinets out when the base is destroyed or the cavity cannot be dried in place. Countertops can often be lifted and reset.

Tile on a wet subfloor can hide water under it. A moisture meter helps decide whether to lift it. LGR dehumidifiers pull the humidity out of the room so drying does not stall.

What changes the price of kitchen water damage

Kitchens vary more than any other room, so we explain the scope first. These factors change the number:

  • Water category, since a clean supply line differs from a dishwasher drain
  • Cabinet material, such as plywood, particleboard or solid wood
  • Flooring type and whether it must be lifted
  • How many drying days and how much equipment the cavity needs
  • Whether the ceiling below was wet
  • Rebuild scope for cabinets, counters and flooring

How insurance handles a kitchen leak

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance uses sudden and accidental as its standard for burst pipe coverage, and it advises calling your insurer as soon as possible. Kitchen leaks fit that standard when a line splits or a hose pops off. A slow seep under the sink that went on for months often does not.

Check your policy and ask your insurer. Photograph the damage and keep receipts. Standard homeowners policies do not cover flood, so rising groundwater is a different question from a plumbing leak.

Mold in the dark cabinet and other mistakes

A dark, closed cabinet is where mold likes to grow once it gets wet. The EPA says drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours usually prevents mold growth. After that window, check behind the cabinet back and under the sink liner.

  • Wiping the cabinet and closing the door on a wet floor
  • Laying new flooring over a subfloor nobody measured
  • Throwing out a cabinet that could have dried in place
  • Keeping the sink trap leak for weeks because it is only a drip

If you see spotting, read about mold remediation. Remediation follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.
