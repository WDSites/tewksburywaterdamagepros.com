Emergency Storm Water Damage in Tewksbury, MA

Wind-driven rain, a limb through the roof or a sump pump that stopped when the power did.

Storm water damage in Tewksbury, MA comes from water that entered through an opening, not from a river rising. A limb punches a hole in the roof, wind drives rain around a window, siding peels back, or a sump pump goes quiet when the grid drops. The water is real, and it needs to come out fast.

This page is about water that arrived from above or through a failed opening. If water is rising from outside, see flood damage restoration. If storm water is inside your home right now, call (978) 252-8637.

Wet carpet, a dripping ceiling and a silent sump pump after the storm

The signs vary with the storm. After a thunderstorm you might find a damp window wall and a wet sill. After a nor'easter, a ceiling stain, a soaked attic or an inch of water in the basement where the sump pump stopped.

These are different openings with the same result, which is water inside materials that were not built to be wet. That is the work of storm water damage restoration: remove the water, map where it went and dry the structure before mold gets a start.

How storms get water inside

Think of the building envelope as a set of openings that can fail. Roofs lose shingles and flashing in wind. Windows and doors leak where caulk has aged. Siding and trim let rain behind the house wrap. Chimneys and vents are open to the weather.

Fallen limbs are the severe case. A branch through the roof opens the attic and the room below at once. For that, a roofer or carpenter closes the opening and we deal with the wet insulation, drywall and floors. Roof-specific problems have their own page on roof leak water damage.

Safety first: downed lines, standing water and power loss

Storms add dangers that a plain leak does not. Treat any downed wire as live, stay far away and call the utility or 911. Do not drive over or walk near a line lying in a yard or on a car.

  • Never step into water if a basement outlet, panel or appliance could be energized.
  • Turn off power to the affected area at the breaker panel only if you can reach it dry.
  • Run a generator outdoors, away from windows, and never plug it into a wall outlet.
  • Stay out of rooms with a sagging ceiling or a roof opening.

MEMA advises people to consider a generator and to learn its safe use before an outage, not during one. Read the manual while the lights are on.

Document the damage before anything moves

Take photos and video of every wet room, the opening where water came in and any fallen tree or limb. Photograph serial numbers on damaged electronics and keep wet items you plan to claim until your insurer has seen them or approved disposal.

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance advises calling your insurer as soon as possible, not making permanent repairs before the insurer inspects, and saving receipts for temporary ones. A tarp, a plywood board or a rental pump counts as temporary.

Our process for storm water: extract, map, dry

Storm water that enters a house is clean at first (Category 1 under ANSI/IICRC S500), but ground water carrying soil or sewage is Category 3. We check the source before anything else because it decides what can be dried and what must be discarded.

  1. Extract

    Remove standing water with truck-mounted or portable extraction units and submersible pumps.

  2. Map

    Use moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera to trace water behind walls and under floors.

  3. Dry

    Run air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and HEPA air scrubbers where dust or debris was disturbed.

  4. Document

    Record readings each visit so you have a written drying log for your claim.

The method follows the same steps as water mitigation, tuned for openings and debris.

When the sump pump fails during an outage

A sump pump runs on electricity. When a storm takes out power, the pit fills and the pump cannot lift anything. MEMA advises installing sump pumps with back-up power and checking existing ones regularly. It also advises raising furnaces, water heaters and electric panels off the floor where flooding is a risk.

Tewksbury sewer regulations add a rule to know. Sump pumps, foundation drains and roof downspouts may not connect to the sanitary sewer, so a pump discharge hose must run outdoors. Water in a basement is covered in more depth on our basement water damage page.

How heavy a Tewksbury storm can get

In a National Weather Service public information statement for August 8, 2023, a weather station near Tewksbury reported 5.61 inches of rain in 24 hours. That is one station on one day, but it shows how fast a single storm can overwhelm gutters, window wells and drains.

The National Weather Service defines a Flood Advisory as inundation of low-lying, poor-drainage areas, and a Flood Warning as overflow that causes or will cause damage or a threat to life. A Flash Flood Warning means a rapid, extreme flow of high water into a normally dry area. When you see a warning, move valuables up and clear the drains.

FEMA records show Middlesex County was designated for Individual Assistance in the October 2005 storms, FEMA-1614-DR. The record covers the county as a whole and does not name Tewksbury.

What changes the cost of storm water work

Storm jobs vary more than most because the opening matters as much as the water. The scope depends on these factors:

  • Water category, which depends on where the water came from
  • Depth and area of water, and how many rooms it crossed
  • Materials affected, such as plaster, drywall, carpet pad, hardwood or insulation
  • Drying days and equipment
  • Debris, tree or roof opening that needs coordination with other trades
  • Rebuild scope

Insurance fit and the flood line

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance states that standard homeowners and commercial policies do not cover flood damage, though some coverage may exist with a sump pump failure or water backup endorsement. Wind-driven rain through a storm-made opening is often handled differently than rising water, so ask your insurer which one applies.

FEMA notes that flood insurance policies from the National Flood Insurance Program typically take effect 30 days after purchase, so buying coverage when a storm is in the forecast is too late. Check your declarations page now, and look up your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.
