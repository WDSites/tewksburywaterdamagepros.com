Carpet Water Damage in Tewksbury, MA

Wet wall-to-wall carpet and pad: what can be dried in place, what comes out, and how the subfloor gets dry.

☎ Call (978) 252-8637

Carpet water damage in Tewksbury, MA is one of the most common calls a restoration dispatcher takes, because a leak that looks small on the surface usually hides a lot of water underneath. Wall-to-wall carpet sits on a pad that acts like a sponge, and the pad sits on a subfloor that can stay wet for days.

The honest answer to whether your carpet can be saved depends on where the water came from and how long it sat. We explain that before any work starts, and we work with your plumber or appliance repair person, who fix the source. For a whole-room view of the process, see our water mitigation page.

Squishy carpet, damp smell, rippling or darker patches

Press your heel into the carpet and look for water welling around it. A squishy feel, a damp or sour smell, rippled edges along the wall or a darker patch on the surface all point to water in the pad. A musty smell several days later suggests it never fully dried.

That is where carpet water damage restoration starts: finding out how far the water spread under the surface, then deciding what gets extracted, dried in place or removed. A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera show the wet zone, which is usually larger than the stain.

Water category decides whether carpet is dried or removed

ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration, sorts water into three categories. Category 1 is clean supply line water. Category 2 has significant contamination, such as washing machine or dishwasher discharge. Category 3 is grossly contaminated, like sewage or river flood water.

Water categoryTypical sourceUsual carpet outcome
Category 1Supply line, clean overflowExtract, often dry carpet in place if acted on quickly
Category 2Washing machine or dishwasher dischargeCarpet may be cleaned and dried or removed, depending on time and extent
Category 3Sewage, river flood waterCarpet and pad removed and discarded

Time moves a category up. A clean spill that sits for days is no longer clean water, so call early.

Why the pad usually comes out

Padding is thick foam or fiber that holds water like a sponge and dries slowly under carpet. Even when the carpet itself can be dried in place, the pad is often removed, cut into strips and discarded, because drying it under a sealed carpet is slow and uncertain. New pad is cheaper than the problems that follow a pad that stays damp.

For sewage, MassDEP is blunt: after a sewage backup, discard carpet padding, upholstered furniture and mattresses. The EPA also notes that hidden mold can grow on the underside of carpets and pads, which is another reason to look beneath the surface before you decide.

Safety steps for wet carpet: the first hour

Stop the water first. Close the shutoff for the fixture or appliance, or the main if you cannot find it. Then protect yourself and your equipment.

  • Switch off power to the room if outlets or cords are in wet carpet and you can reach the panel dry. Otherwise, call an electrician.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on wet carpet. A wet-dry vacuum is built for it, but keep it away from wet outlets.
  • Lift furniture legs onto foil or plastic so wood and stain do not transfer.
  • Keep children and pets off the wet area if the water was not clean.
  • Call 911 for a gas smell, an electrical burning smell or standing water near a live panel.

Pull up loose rugs and hang them to dry. Photograph the room before anything moves.

How we dry wet carpet, step by step

The process is built around moving water out and moving air in. Not every step applies to every room, and your scope will say which do.

  1. Extract

    An extraction wand with a truck-mounted or portable unit pulls water from the carpet. A weighted wand pass over the pad area removes more than a mop can.

  2. Lift and check

    The carpet is pulled from the tack strips along one wall and floated or peeled back, so the pad and subfloor are exposed and read with a moisture meter.

  3. Remove the pad

    Wet pad is cut up and bagged. Tack strips and baseboard that stayed wet may be removed too.

  4. Dry

    Air movers blow across the floating carpet and the subfloor, while LGR dehumidifiers take moisture out of the air.

  5. Verify

    Daily moisture readings continue until the subfloor and any wood trim reach a dry target, then carpet is reset or replaced.

Floating carpet, subfloor drying and odor

When carpet is floated, it is lifted at one edge, supported on air movers and left to dry while the subfloor beneath is dried separately. This often saves carpet that would otherwise be replaced, but it works only when the water was clean enough and the carpet was lifted early.

A plywood subfloor holds water longer than the carpet does, and a concrete slab behaves differently still. The EPA says increasing air circulation and temperature speeds drying, and that professionals often use large fans and dehumidifiers for the job. If a sour or mildew smell lingers after drying, the cause is usually something still wet below the surface, not a need for more air freshener.

Area rugs and basement carpet

An area rug is a different case. Small rugs can often be taken outside, rinsed, hung and dried, while a large wool or silk rug may need professional cleaning. If it sat in contaminated water, plan to discard it.

Basement carpet is a harder call because it often sits on a concrete slab with a thin pad. Concrete holds moisture, and a basement that was flooded is likely to stay damp for longer than an upstairs bedroom. See our basement water damage page for the slab and wall side of that job, and ask whether the carpet was glued down. A finished basement with a moisture issue is a common reason to consider removing carpet rather than drying it.

What changes the cost of carpet water damage

We do not quote a flat number for carpet because the variables are large. Here is what drives the price:

  • Water category, which decides whether carpet is dried in place or discarded
  • Square footage of wet carpet and pad, and how far the water traveled under walls
  • Carpet type, whether glued down, and how hard the pad is to access
  • Drying days and the number of air movers and dehumidifiers
  • Subfloor condition and any baseboard or drywall that needs work
  • Replacement carpet, pad and install if the existing carpet cannot be kept

Insurance and mistakes to avoid

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a supply line failure, is often covered under a homeowners policy, while gradual leaks and flooding often are not. Check your policy or ask your insurer. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance advises calling your insurer as soon as possible and not having permanent repairs made until the insurer has inspected.

The biggest mistake is waiting. The EPA recommends drying wet areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, and carpet that sits wet past that window may need to come out. Do not cover wet carpet with a rug, and do not run a heater alone. If dark spots appear, see mold remediation. Neighbors in Chelmsford and Lowell can call the same dispatch line.
