South Tewksbury is the southern part of town, toward the Wilmington line. The Town's master plan describes it as more densely developed than other parts of Tewksbury, with many modest and somewhat smaller homes and a substantial share of the town's older housing stock.

Older and smaller houses can mean older plumbing, older basements and tighter rooms to dry. This page covers what that means for a leak and where the nearby water context comes from.

Small lots and older houses

The Town's open space plan notes 1920s lots that are very small, between Main, South and Brown Streets and the Wilmington town line. Close houses and short setbacks leave little room to move equipment, so we plan hose runs and drying equipment with your layout in mind.

Older homes may have galvanized or copper supply lines and cast-iron drains. We do not assume yours do. A plumber confirms the pipe, and we handle the wet materials, so see water damage cleanup for how removal and sanitizing work.

Wet ground near the Shawsheen River

Recent local projects show the water context here. A Tewksbury news report from 2023 describes funded floodplain work on Poplar Street, where the town says the street is in a floodplain, and on Mollie Drive, which borders the Shawsheen River. The stated goal on Mollie Drive is more flood storage capacity.

These projects are about public land, not about your house. They are a reason to check your sump pump, your basement floor and your window wells before a heavy rain. If outdoor water enters the house, it is treated as contaminated under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. See basement water damage and flood damage restoration. The Shawsheen Street area sits just to the east.

Safety steps in the first hour

  1. Turn off the main water valve if a pipe or appliance is the source.
  2. Do not operate the breaker, switches or outlets while standing in water. Call an electrician or your utility.
  3. If water is rising from a drain, a sump pit or outside, stay out of it and do not run fans.
  4. Keep children and pets away from the wet area.
  5. Photograph the water line and damaged items before moving anything.

Then call (978) 252-8637. Tell the dispatcher your street, which level is wet and whether the water is still coming in.

Documentation for your insurer

Flood damage and plumbing damage are often treated differently, and your policy decides what is covered. We do not promise coverage. We take photos, record moisture readings and write down what was removed, so you have a clear record for your adjuster.

Checking mold afterward

A basement that stays damp can grow mold on walls and stored items. If you smell it or see it after drying, mention it. Mold is its own scope, described on the mold remediation page.
