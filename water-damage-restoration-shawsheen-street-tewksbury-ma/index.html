Water Damage Restoration in Shawsheen Street, Tewksbury, MA

Heath Brook, the Shawsheen River and low flat ground give this southeast part of town its water story.

The Shawsheen Street area is in the southeast part of Tewksbury, along the Shawsheen River. The Massachusetts Historical Commission's 1980 report says initial settlement was scattered through the southeast part of town, and places a burying ground at Shawsheen and Main Streets.

Of the places in town, this is where the public record on water is strongest. The details below come from town and watershed sources and describe public places, not individual homes.

Heath Brook and the low flat ground

The Town's open space plan says Heath Brook, Sutton Brook and Strongwater Brook flow to the Shawsheen. A town climate resilience page by the Merrimack River Watershed Council shows Heath Brook flooding at Shawsheen and Main Streets and says stormwater flooding is most common in flat and low-lying areas.

It names undersized culverts, poor drainage and hard surfaces as causes. For a homeowner, that points to a clear habit: watch the basement after a heavy storm, and keep gutters and drains clear.

The August 2023 storm

Local news reported that Heath Brook Elementary School on Shawsheen Street flooded during more than five inches of rain on August 7 to 9, 2023, and flooded again on August 18. The National Weather Service rainfall report is the source cited for the storm.

That was a school building, not a home statistic. It does show what a heavy rain can do on low ground here, so our storm water damage page explains how wind-driven rain and runoff are handled. Next door, South Tewksbury shares the same river and the same Wilmington line, and we also serve Wilmington.

Flood water is handled as contaminated water

Water that came from a brook, a street or the ground is treated as contaminated under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. Carpet, padding and drywall that soaked in it usually come out, and hard surfaces are cleaned and disinfected. See flood damage restoration and water damage cleanup.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified here. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address, and ask your agent about separate flood coverage.

Safety steps in the first hour

  1. Stay out of flood water. It can hide sharp objects and may be contaminated.
  2. Do not touch the breaker, switches or outlets while standing in water. Call an electrician or your utility.
  3. Do not run fans and do not try to clean up sewage or outdoor flood water yourself.
  4. Shut off the main water valve if a pipe is also involved.
  5. Take photos and video of the water line before anything is moved.

Then call (978) 252-8637 and tell the dispatcher your street, the floor affected and whether the water is still rising.

Insurance and mold after a flood

Standard homeowners policies often treat flood differently from plumbing failures, so check your declarations page. We document the loss and do not decide coverage.

Mold can follow if materials stay wet. It is a separate scope, described on the mold remediation page.
