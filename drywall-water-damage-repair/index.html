Drywall Water Damage Repair in Tewksbury, MA

Wet or stained wall board, from the moisture check through cutting out, drying the cavity, patching and paint.

☎ Call (978) 252-8637

Drywall water damage repair in Tewksbury, MA begins with a question most homeowners skip: is the wall still wet? Gypsum board soaks up water quickly and loses strength as it does, so a stained, bubbled or soft wall is telling you something is wet inside the cavity.

The plumber fixes the pipe first. After that, we measure, cut out what cannot be saved, dry the cavity, and rebuild the wall. If you also have a ceiling issue, that has its own treatment elsewhere on this site. This page stays on walls, and for the wider rebuild, see water damage repair.

Stained, bubbling, soft or crumbling wall board

A tan or brown stain, paint that bubbles, a seam that opens, a baseboard that lifts or a wall that feels soft to a thumb press are typical signs. A smell of damp paper is another. Drywall wicks upward from a wet floor and downward from a leak behind it.

The surface rarely shows the full wet area. A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera read the board and the studs behind it, which tells us whether we are patching a spot or dealing with a wall that needs to open. This is the point where a stain becomes a repair job.

Below bathrooms, behind kitchens, after floods

The walls we hear about most are the ones below a bathroom, where a tub overflow or a supply leak soaks the board from above. Next are walls behind a kitchen sink, dishwasher or refrigerator line, where a slow drip can stay hidden for weeks.

After a flood or a burst pipe, water enters at the bottom and wicks upward. A flood cut, made along a level line above the water mark, removes the wet lower section so the cavity can dry. It is often two feet or less, but the moisture reading sets the cut, not a guess. For kitchen-specific jobs, see our kitchen water damage page.

Safety steps for wet drywall: the first hour

Stop the water, then work the wall carefully. A wall that has been wet for hours can hide energized wiring and weak board.

  • Shut off the fixture valve or the main if you cannot find the source.
  • Switch off the breaker for the wall circuits if outlets or switches are wet and the panel is dry to reach. If not, call an electrician.
  • Do not poke holes or pull the board until you know what is behind it.
  • Move furniture away from the wall, and lift baseboard heat covers if they are dripping.
  • If the water was from a sewer or flood, treat the wall as contaminated and call a professional.

The EPA advises hiring a contaminated-water professional when sewage or other contaminated water was the source. Call 911 for sparks, a burning smell or a gas odor.

How the repair is done, in order

The sequence matters because new board over a wet cavity brings the problem back.

  1. Measure

    Moisture meter and thermal camera mark the wet extent on the face and up the stud bay.

  2. Cut out

    The wet or crumbling board is removed in clean rectangles. Wet insulation is pulled and bagged.

  3. Dry the cavity

    Air movers push air into the open bay, and LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture from the room. Studs and plates are read until dry.

  4. Patch

    New board is cut to fit, screwed to dry framing, and the seams are taped and coated with joint compound.

  5. Sand, prime, paint

    Joints are sanded smooth, stain-blocking primer seals the area, and the wall is painted to match.

Fresh insulation goes in before the new board if the old batt was wet.

Older homes: ask about lead and asbestos before sanding

If your house was built before 1978, ask the contractor about lead-safe work and testing before anyone sands or cuts. The EPA lead renovation rule requires that anyone paid to disturb painted surfaces in a pre-1978 home follow its lead-safe requirements, and the Massachusetts Department of Public Health notes that disturbing more than 6 square feet of painted interior surface triggers lead-safe renovation rules.

MassDEP lists plaster and wallboard joint compound among suspect asbestos-containing materials, and requires notification at least ten working days before removal or disturbance of regulated material. The homeowner exemption is narrow. In an older house, ask about testing before the wall is opened, and ask the Tewksbury Building Department whether your repair needs a permit.

How long drywall takes to dry, and why timing matters

The Massachusetts Department of Public Health says wet areas should be dried completely within 24 hours, and the state housing code calls for areas exposed to leaks or floods to be dried within 48 hours. The EPA says materials dried within 24 to 48 hours usually avoid mold growth.

That is why we move fast on a wet wall. A board that sits wet past that window is more likely to need replacement, and porous materials may have to be replaced if they become moldy. If you see dark spotting, mold remediation is the path, and S520 is the published standard for it.

What changes the cost of a drywall repair

Costs follow the scope, not the stain. These are the factors:

  • Water category and whether the wall needs contaminated-water handling
  • How many wall sections, rooms and stud bays are wet
  • Insulation, framing or wiring that must be dried or replaced
  • Drying days and equipment
  • Lead or asbestos testing on older homes
  • Taping, texture matching, priming and painting, including whole-wall paint for a clean finish

Insurance fit and common patching mistakes

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, and gradual leaks often are not. Flood damage usually needs separate coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer before permanent repairs begin.

The usual mistakes: painting over a stain without drying behind it, closing a wall before readings are normal, and sanding old paint without checking for lead. Plumbers fix the pipe first, and the wall comes next. We also take calls from Andover and Billerica.
