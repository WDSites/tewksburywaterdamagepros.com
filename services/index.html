Which service do I need?

Water damage rarely shows up as one clean problem. A split pipe becomes standing water, then wet drywall, then a musty closet. We organized the services four ways, so you can start at the page that matches what you can see: the cause, the room, the material or the storm.

If water is still moving, call first and read after. The dispatch line is open 24 hours a day, and every page explains what the work involves and how scope and price are set.

Water is still arriving or standing

Start with emergency water removal, then drying through water mitigation.

Water stopped but things are soaked

Cleanup removes what cannot be saved. See water damage cleanup for the sorting rules.

The room is dry and needs rebuilding

Water damage repair covers walls, ceilings, floors and paint.

Water came from outside or a drain

Flood damage restoration treats it as contaminated water.

By cause

Emergency Frozen Pipe Water Damage

A split from a hard freeze that shows itself at the thaw, in garages, exterior walls and unheated additions.

Emergency Water Heater Leak Cleanup

A leaking tank or fitting, usually in a basement or utility closet, and the wet flooring and walls around it.

Appliance Leak Water Damage

Washing machine hoses, dishwashers, ice maker lines and dehumidifiers that fail while no one is looking.

AC Leak Water Damage

Clogged condensate lines and overflowing drain pans in attics, closets and above ceilings during humid weather.

Emergency Slab Leak Water Damage

A supply line leaking under a concrete slab, which is uncommon here but real in slab-on-grade homes and additions.

Emergency Roof Leak Water Damage

Wind-driven rain, flashing failures and ice dams that send water through the attic into ceilings.

Emergency Sewage Backup Cleanup

Drain and sewer backups that bring contaminated water into a basement, handled with protective gear and disposal.

By room or place

Kitchen Water Damage

Sink lines, dishwashers and the cabinets and subfloor that hide a slow leak.

Basement Water Damage

Groundwater, sump pump failure and seepage in finished and unfinished basements.

Emergency Crawl Space Water Damage

Standing water, wet insulation and damp framing under additions and some older homes.

Commercial Water Damage

Water extraction and drying for commercial spaces, with a written scope before work starts.

By material

Carpet Water Damage

When wet carpet and pad can be dried in place and when they have to come out.

Drywall Water Damage Repair

Wet, stained or crumbling wall board, from the moisture check to the final coat of paint.

Storm damage

Emergency Storm Water Damage

Nor'easter and thunderstorm damage: wind-driven rain, fallen limbs and water that came in from above.

By stage of the job

Water Damage Repair

Rebuilding after drying: drywall, plaster ceilings, hardwood and finishes. If the room is still wet, water mitigation comes first.

Emergency Water Mitigation

The first 24 to 72 hours: moisture mapping, extraction and structural drying so the structure does not keep absorbing water.

Emergency Water Damage Cleanup

Removing soaked and contaminated materials, sanitizing and deodorizing, with a written list of what was removed.

Emergency Water Removal

Pumps and extraction units for standing water, burst pipes, appliance failures and flooded levels.

Emergency Flood Damage Restoration

Rising water from rivers, heavy rain and overloaded drains, handled as contaminated water under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.

Mold Remediation

Source-first mold removal after leaks, with containment and answers about testing and clearance for a sale or loan.
