Appliance Leak Water Damage in Tewksbury, MA

Washers, dishwashers, ice makers and dehumidifiers leak into floors, cabinets and the ceilings below. We find the wet material and dry it.

☎ Call (978) 252-8637

Appliance leak water damage in Tewksbury, MA usually starts small. A hose clamp loosens, a drain line slips off its standpipe, or an ice maker line weeps behind the unit. The water has nowhere to go but under flooring and into the wall next to it.

A first-floor laundry room may only mean wet vinyl and a damp baseboard. A second-floor laundry room is different, because water drips into the ceiling below and spreads. For a deeper look at the drying side, see water mitigation.

Wet floor near the machine, brown ceiling stain below: what it means

A damp spot near a washer or dishwasher is rarely just a spill. If the floor feels soft, the baseboard has swollen, or a ceiling in the room below shows a tan ring, water has been traveling for a while. Appliance leaks tend to be slow and hidden, so by the time you notice them, material underneath may already be soaked.

Callers often tell us the machine itself still works fine. That is the trap. A washing machine can run perfectly while a corroded fitting at the wall box feeds water into the stud bay each cycle. Moving the appliance away from the wall and checking the floor behind it often reveals the real extent.

Safety steps in the first hour

  • Turn off the appliance and unplug it only if the cord and outlet are dry.
  • Shut the stop valves behind the washer, under the sink or on the ice maker line. If you cannot find them, close the main water shutoff.
  • Do not touch outlets, power strips or cords that sit in standing water. Switch off the breaker for that circuit first.
  • Do not lift a heavy washer from a wet floor alone. Wait for help rather than risk a slip.
  • Photograph the leak, the appliance label and the wet areas before anything moves.

If water is pouring through a ceiling light fixture, leave the room and turn off the breaker for that circuit. Call 911 for any sparking or burning smell.

Which appliance leaked and what that means for the water

Washing machine

Supply hose bursts push clean water fast. Drain hose and standpipe overflows carry gray water that ANSI/IICRC S500 classes as Category 2.

Dishwasher

Door gasket failures leak at the front. Supply and drain connections leak under the unit and soak the cabinet base and subfloor.

Ice maker line

A pinched or cracked plastic line behind a refrigerator can drip for days. The refrigerator is one item here, not the whole story.

Dehumidifier tank

A full tank that overflows or a drain hose that kinks puts water on basement floors and nearby cardboard.

Utility sink

A loose trap or a cracked supply hose under the sink soaks the cabinet and the flooring around it.

Gray water from a washer or dishwasher drain has been used, so it carries soap and food residue. That changes how porous materials like carpet pad and insulation are handled.

How we dry an appliance leak

We start with a moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera, which show cool wet zones under floors and behind walls without cutting anything open. Then we decide what gets pulled and what can stay.

  1. Extract standing water with portable extraction units and wet vacuums.
  2. Pull the toe kick, baseboard or the bottom of a wall where water wicked in.
  3. Lift wet carpet pad or floating flooring planks if they are trapping moisture.
  4. Set air movers across the surface and an LGR dehumidifier to hold humidity down.
  5. Re-read moisture daily and move equipment until the readings are normal.

Upstairs laundry leaks need one more step: we open the ceiling below at a small spot if a thermal scan shows trapped water, so the cavity can dry instead of sagging.

What changes the cost of an appliance leak cleanup

The price depends on what the water touched, and not on which appliance caused it. The main drivers are these:

  • Water category, clean supply line water versus gray drain water
  • How many rooms or floors the water traveled through
  • Flooring type, such as vinyl, laminate, tile or carpet with pad
  • Cabinet and drywall removal, and how many days of drying equipment are needed
  • Rebuild scope after drying is finished

How insurance sees an appliance leak

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance tells policyholders to call the insurer or agent as soon as possible with the policy number. It also says not to make permanent repairs before the insurer has inspected, and to save every receipt, including those for temporary repairs. Follow those steps in that order.

Sudden and accidental discharge, such as a burst hose, is often covered. A slow drip behind a dishwasher that went on for months often is not. Appliance coverage differs from policy to policy, so check yours and ask your insurer. We document readings and photos in writing that you can share with your claim.

Mistakes to avoid after an appliance leak

  • Wiping the surface and calling it done while the subfloor stays wet
  • Running a fan alone, which moves humid air without removing the water
  • Replacing a hose and leaving the wall bay closed and damp
  • Pushing the washer back into place before checking behind it
  • Throwing away the old hose before the insurer sees it

The EPA says drying wet areas and items within 24 to 48 hours usually prevents mold growth. We do not state a replacement interval for hoses, because we have no official Massachusetts guidance on that. Instead, look at your connections once a season for cracks, bulges and rust at the fittings.
