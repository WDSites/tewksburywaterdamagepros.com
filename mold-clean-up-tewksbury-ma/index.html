Mold Clean Up in Tewksbury, MA

A practical way to sort a small wipe-down job from one that needs containment and a crew.

By the Tewksbury Water Damage Pros team · 2026

☎ Call (978) 252-8637

Mold clean up in Tewksbury, MA starts with two questions: where is the moisture coming from, and how big is the patch? A small surface spot on a hard material can often be cleaned with detergent and water. Anything over 10 square feet, or anything hidden, is a job for professionals.

Most calls we hear describe the same handful of situations. A closet wall under an upstairs leak, a bathroom ceiling corner, a drip line from an air conditioner, the back of a couch that sat against an outside wall, a corner of lifted carpet. Here is how to size each one up.

Fuzzy spots in the house: five usual places

Closet after an upstairs leak

Water follows a pipe or a wall cavity and shows up at the closet baseboard or shelf. The growth is on the surface, but the drywall behind it is probably wet.

Bathroom ceiling above the shower

Steam condenses on the coolest surface. If an exhaust fan is weak or vents into the attic, the corner stays damp and darkens.

AC condensate line backing up

A clogged drain pan or line drips onto a ceiling or air handler closet. Look for staining under the unit and a wet pan.

Behind pulled-away furniture

Air barely moves behind a dresser against an exterior wall. Cold wall plus indoor humidity gives you a damp patch you did not see for months.

Under lifted carpet

A damp pad holds water against the tack strip and subfloor. If the carpet smells sour when you peel back a corner, the pad is wet.

Fix the moisture first, then decide on the cleanup

The Massachusetts Department of Public Health homeowner guide says to find the water source and fix it, or mold will not stop growing. A cleaned wall that stays wet is a wall that grows mold again. So the first job is a plumber for the supply line, a technician for the AC drain, a fan for the bathroom, a gap behind the furniture.

Then dry. The guide says wet areas should be completely dried in 24 hours, and under the Massachusetts housing code areas exposed to leaks must be dried within 48 hours. Fans, open windows and a dehumidifier are the home tools. If a surface is still damp after two days, bring in a moisture meter reading.

What not to do

  • Do not paint over it. Paint hides the stain and leaves the moisture and the growth underneath.
  • Do not scrub with a dry brush or sweep. It throws spores into the air and across the room.
  • Do not mix bleach with ammonia. The fumes are dangerous. If you use bleach at all, the DPH guide caps it at 1 cup of laundry bleach per gallon of water.
  • Do not aim a fan at the patch before you have decided whether to call. It can push spores into other rooms.
  • Do not disturb more than 6 square feet of painted interior surface without asking about lead-safe rules.

When a lender or a buyer wants it cleared

Callers often tell us a lender or a buyer wants mold cleared before a sale. In that case, the usual step after remediation is independent clearance testing: a third party who did not do the cleanup inspects the area and may sample air or surfaces, then reports whether it meets the criteria set for the job.

Independent matters, since the company that removed the mold should not be the one grading its own work. We cannot say whether any particular lender or buyer will accept a given report, so ask them what they require in writing before you schedule anything.
