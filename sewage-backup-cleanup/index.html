Emergency Sewage Backup Cleanup in Tewksbury, MA

Sewage in the house is contaminated water. Stay out, stop using drains and call for a professional cleanup.

☎ Call (978) 252-8637

Sewage backup cleanup in Tewksbury, MA is not a mop job. Water that comes up a floor drain, toilet or tub is Category 3 under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, meaning grossly contaminated. The EPA advises hiring a professional experienced with contaminated water.

Callers describe a basement drain that bubbled and a gray puddle that kept spreading. If that is your house, stop using water and keep everyone out of the wet area.

Gurgling drains, water in the floor drain, or a toilet that will not flush

  • Gurgling from a drain when a washer or sink runs
  • Water or sludge in a basement floor drain or lowest tub
  • Several fixtures backing up at once
  • A sewage smell with no visible source

Stop using sinks, tubs and washers, because every gallon has to go somewhere. Once sewage reaches the floor, the job is Category 3 removal and disinfection. Our flood damage restoration page gives a short summary, and this page covers it in full.

Your lateral or the town main? Who decides

The backup may be in your building lateral, the pipe from house to street, or in the Town's sewer main. You usually cannot tell by looking.

The Tewksbury Sewer Regulations say the owner keeps the private sewerage works and the building service lateral in working order. The Superintendent governs the Town's sewerage works. A licensed plumber or the Town decides which pipe is blocked, not us.

The Water and Sewer Division lists office and field hours of Monday to Friday, 7 am to 3:30 pm, and posts an emergency line on its Town page. Call it if neighbors are also affected, then call us to clean up.

Safety steps for the first hour

The MassDEP home care guide says people with weakened immune systems face higher infection risk, so keep children, pets and ill family members out.

  1. Stop using all water and do not flush.
  2. Wear boots, gloves and eye protection if you must enter.
  3. Cut power to the area only from dry ground. Otherwise call your electric utility or 911.
  4. Do not run a household vacuum or a box fan. They spread droplets.
  5. Photograph damage before moving anything.

What a Category 3 cleanup looks like, step by step

Crews arrive in disposable suits, boots, gloves, eye protection and respirators.

  1. Contain and extract

    Extraction units pull water into sealed containers while doors and vents are isolated.

  2. Remove porous materials

    Carpet, pad, wet insulation and wall bottoms are bagged and hauled.

  3. Wash, then disinfect

    Detergent first, then a disinfectant labeled for the job.

  4. Dry the structure

    Air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and HEPA air scrubbers run while meters track the framing.

  5. Check and clear odor

    Anything still smelling is removed or treated again.

What gets thrown away and what can be saved

MassDEP says to discard upholstered furniture, mattresses, carpet padding, food, cosmetics, medicines, toys and cardboard that touched the water. Porous items soak up contaminants.

Concrete, tile, sealed wood and metal can often be washed and dried. For home disinfecting, the state guide gives one quarter cup of household bleach per gallon of water, never undiluted. Never mix bleach with other cleaners. List and photograph items before bagging, since your insurer will ask.

  • Carpet, drywall and insulation to remove and haul
  • Days of drying and air filtration
  • Rebuild scope afterward

Insurance, septic systems and what to avoid

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says damage from backed-up drains or sewers is not covered under most standard homeowners policies. A water backup endorsement may exist, so check your declarations page. We cannot promise coverage.

Sump pumps, foundation drains and downspouts may not connect to the sanitary sewer under Tewksbury's regulations, so do not rig one as a fix. On septic, Title 5 generally requires an inspection within two years before a sale, by a MassDEP-approved inspector.

Tewksbury's sewage flows to the Lowell Regional Wastewater Utility, and we also cover Lowell and Billerica. For a flooded lower level, see basement water damage.
