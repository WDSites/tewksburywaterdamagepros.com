Standing water, sagging insulation and damp joists under your floor need pumping and measured drying.

Crawl space water damage in Tewksbury, MA is less common than the basement kind. The U.S. Energy Information Administration's 2020 survey counted about 3.30 million New England homes with a basement against 0.34 million with none. A crawl space is usually found under an addition, a porch, or a smaller older home. That is regional, not a Tewksbury count.

Nobody goes down there, so water can sit for weeks. If your lowest level is a full basement, see basement water damage instead.

Cold floors, a musty smell or sagging insulation: what is going on below

Water under a house leaves clues upstairs. Floors feel cold or springy over one section, and a damp earthy smell comes up through registers.

  • Fiberglass insulation sagging or lying on the ground
  • Standing water, or soil that is dark and soft
  • Dark staining on joists or subfloor
  • A vapor barrier torn, pooled with water or pulled loose

The trade term is water mitigation: find the water, pump it, remove what cannot dry and dry the rest. Our water mitigation page covers the stages.

Why crawl spaces take on water around here

Three routes cover most cases: runoff entering at the foundation, a leak from a line inside the space, and condensation on cold surfaces. Each needs a different trade to fix.

Massachusetts residential code R401.3, published on Mass.gov, requires lots to be graded to drain surface water away from foundation walls. Look at the soil along your wall after the next storm, because regrading beats buying drying equipment that gets re-wetted.

The Town's Living With Wetlands guide says land within 100 feet of wetlands is regulated, with separate rules within 200 feet of a river, perennial stream or vernal pool. Ask the Conservation Commission before regrading, and do not assume your contractor will pull permits.

Safety steps before anyone goes into the crawl space

  1. Shut off the water at the main if you suspect a pipe, and cut power to the area from dry footing.
  2. Do not enter if water stands near outlets, junction boxes, a furnace or a water heater.
  3. Never go in alone, and tell someone where you are.
  4. Smell gas or hear a hiss? Leave and call the gas utility or 911.

A crawl space is a confined space with low clearance and exposed wiring. A licensed electrician or plumber fixes the source, and we handle the water around it. If the water came from a drain line, see sewage backup cleanup.

How crews pump, remove and dry a wet crawl space

The work is slow because crews drag equipment in on their knees.

  1. Pump the standing water

    Submersible pumps send it through a hose to a spot that drains away from the house.

  2. Meter the structure

    Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera show where the wood is wet.

  3. Remove wet insulation

    Saturated fiberglass will not dry in place, so it is bagged out.

  4. Dry the wood and air

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run, with ducting for tight corners.

  5. Document the vapor barrier

    A displaced sheet is noted for the contractor who restores it.

We follow ANSI/IICRC S500 for water damage.

Humidity, mold and the vapor barrier question

The Massachusetts Department of Public Health mold guide says to find the water source and fix it or mold will not stop growing. In a crawl space the dry-out and the moisture fix need to happen together. The EPA says drying within 24 to 48 hours usually prevents growth.

We do not diagnose health effects. The DPH guide says professionals handle mold-affected areas over 10 square feet, and remediation follows the S520 standard. See mold remediation for how to vet a company, since Massachusetts requires no license for it.

What drives the cost of a crawl space dry-out

Access comes first. A space you can crawl into costs less labor than one needing a hatch widened.

  • Volume of standing water and how long it sat
  • Groundwater, pipe leak or contaminated water
  • Insulation and vapor barrier to remove and haul
  • Joist and subfloor area to dry
  • Days of equipment and rebuild scope
